carTOONography

Themed maps for curious kids: print them, explore them, and see what you can find.

Each map has a game hidden in it, from exploring London and spotting deer to hunting for clownfish to collecting hellos and finding places in a 3D Kingston. They’re the little sisters of my cARTography maps, and more are on the way.

🎄 Santa’s Christmas Eve journey

Santa delivers presents when midnight arrives, but midnight doesn’t arrive everywhere at once. It starts at Kiritimati in the middle of the Pacific and travels west around the world, one time zone at a time, taking 25 hours to reach Pago Pago. Follow Santa to 18 places, each with a Christmas fact.

🎅 Help Santa deliver   Download the A3 print (PDF)

In the game, kids help Santa by tapping the place where midnight arrives next. If they pick a place too early, it tells them what time it still is there.

Off to Lapland?

Where to go in Finnish Lapland with kids in winter: Santa Claus Village on the Arctic Circle, polar bears at Ranua, the SnowCastle in Kemi, husky rides and the Sámi museum in Inari. Another one from day 10 of the #30DayMapChallenge, made by prompting AI only.

Spot London!

A colourful map of central London with twelve famous places to find, from Big Ben and the London Eye to Tower Bridge and the Gherkin. Each one has a fun fact, and there’s a red bus and a pink boat hiding on the map too.

🎮 Play Spot London   Download the A3 print (PDF)

In the game, the landmarks hide behind question marks. Kids tap each one to collect it in their London passport, then take the quiz: look at the picture and tap where that place really is.

Spot the deer!

Richmond Park and Bushy Park are home to more than 900 red and fallow deer. This storybook map shows where people have really spotted them: the glowing patches are the deer hotspots, built from 683 sightings shared on iNaturalist and with the National Mammal Atlas Project. Ten deer are hiding on the map. Can you find them all?

🦌 Play spot the deer   Download the A3 print (PDF)

In the game, kids zoom into the parks to spot the hidden deer and tick off landmarks in their spotter’s notebook, switch the real sightings between seasons to see where deer go in autumn, and earn a deer spotter badge in the quiz. And remember: always stay at least 50 metres away from real deer. Sightings come from GBIF, using only openly licensed records (list of datasets).

Where do clownfish live?

More than 21,000 real sightings of clownfish, mostly photos people shared on iNaturalist, show where these little fish live: only in the warm Indian and Pacific Oceans, never the Atlantic. Can you find all 12 kinds on the map and tick them off?

🐠 Play find the clownfish   Download the A3 print (PDF)

In the game, kids hunt for 12 hidden clownfish to fill their fish book, then take the quiz: which ocean has no clownfish at all? The sightings come from OBIS, and only openly licensed records are used (list of datasets).

Say hello to the world!

Hello in 26 languages, from Bonjour to こんにちは, made to get kids excited about learning languages. The colours show the writing system, so you can spot which hellos share an alphabet. Tick each one you can say!

🎮 Play the hello game   Download the A3 print (PDF)

In the game you tap a dot to hear hello, say it out loud and collect a stamp in your passport. Then try the quiz: can you find where people say Merhaba?

Kingston, in blocks

A blocky adventure map of Kingston upon Thames, Bushy Park and Hampton Court, built from OpenStreetMap. Find all ten places on the list: the palace, the maze, the deer, the toppled phone boxes and, of course, the ice cream.

🎮 Play the 3D map   Download the A2 print (PDF)

In the 3D version you can spin the world, zoom in, and tap the floating icons to collect all ten. Each one has a fact to discover, and there’s confetti when you find them all. It works on phones and tablets too.
