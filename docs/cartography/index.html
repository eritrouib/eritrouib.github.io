Watercolour cities (2026)

In 2022 I made a land cover map of Gorton and Abbey Hey in Manchester, styled with John Nelson’s watercolour approach in ArcGIS Pro. Years later I recreated the look outside Pro, in Python, starting with Elbasan, Albania, where I’m from, and then Athens. Both use the Copernicus Urban Atlas 2021, reclassified into the same blue, green and grey legend I used in the IGNITION project.

Urban Atlas maps land use, not land cover, so there are judgement calls: Krasta, a wooded hill, has the same code as a small town park.

Old-style Peloponnese (2026)

A few years ago I made an old-style map of the Peloponnese in ArcGIS Pro. I made it again without Pro, building the relief from my own 25 m contours and adding the sea and coastline from EMODnet Bathymetry, on a parchment-style background with old-style lettering.

Prints are available to buy: see the About page to get in touch.

The Netherlands, land of windmills (2026)

The same old-style look as the Peloponnese, but in happier colours. Every one of the 1,231 traditional windmills mapped in OpenStreetMap has its own little windmill on the map. The mint and turquoise land is below sea level: a lot of it was drained by windmills in the first place.
