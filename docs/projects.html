Spatial analysis

15-minute London

2026 · Python, GeoPandas, SciPy, network analysis, Leaflet

How much of London is a 15-minute city on foot? Walking distances along every street, footpath and park path, from 454,115 points on London’s residential streets to the nearest GP, pharmacy, primary school, food shop, park and station, compared with straight-line distance and broken down by borough and deprivation. Click any front door on the dashboard and it walks outwards along the streets in your browser, showing how far you get in 5, 10, 15 and 20 minutes.

Pedestrian casualties on London’s roads

2026 · Python, GeoPandas, OSMnx, PySAL, statsmodels, Leaflet

An open, reproducible analysis of every police-recorded pedestrian casualty in Greater London from 2021 to 2025: network kernel density hotspots, Getis-Ord Gi* clusters, a negative binomial model of the built environment, and drive times to major trauma centres. Includes a spotlight to explore any place, a year-by-year playback, CSV downloads, and an AI assistant that answers questions in plain English by searching the data.

Planning and geospatial tools

Article 4 and constraints checker, Kingston

Web tool · JavaScript, Leaflet, Planning Data API, proj4

A Local Land Charges aid for the Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames. Check a single property by address, postcode or grid reference, or upload a CSV to check hundreds at once. Each location is sent to the government’s Planning Data service and checked for Article 4 directions, conservation areas, listed buildings, tree preservation, green belt, flood zones and SSSIs. Nearby features and boundaries within a chosen tolerance (10, 25 or 50 m) are flagged as borderline, and Article 4 results are cross-checked against Kingston’s own boundaries, with an offline fallback if the service is down.

Retrofit planning checker

Web tool · JavaScript, Leaflet, Planning Data API, HM Land Registry INSPIRE

Can this home have a heat pump, solar panels or insulation without planning permission? Enter an address, postcode or grid reference anywhere in England and, for each common energy improvement, the checker says whether it’s usually permitted development, allowed with limits, needs prior approval, or needs planning permission or listed building consent, with a link to the rule. It checks listed buildings, conservation areas, Article 4 directions, protected trees, flood zones and more across the whole Land Registry plot, adjusts for the type of home, and has a batch mode for whole retrofit programmes. Where a council hasn’t published the data, it says “check with the council” rather than “allowed”.

Planning Data Tracker

Web tool and weekly pipeline · Python, GitHub Actions, Leaflet

Planning tools can only answer from what councils publish, so a “no tree preservation order” can really mean “unknown”. This tracker maps, for all 337 local planning authorities in England, which of 10 council-published datasets have records in their area, from Article 4 and conservation areas to brownfield land and local green spaces, and flags data-quality problems with Article 4 directions. It updates every week, and any council can be re-checked live. It grew out of the Kingston checker, where 113 Article 4 areas turned out not to link to their directions.

Takeaways near schools

Web tool · JavaScript, Leaflet, FSA and DfE open data

Many councils restrict new hot-food takeaways within a set distance of schools. For any council in England, this maps every open school with a zone of your chosen size (200 to 800 m), every takeaway from food hygiene data, and which schools have the most nearby. You choose which school phases count, check whether a proposed site falls inside a zone, and download the results as CSV. It’s clear about its limits, such as straight-line distances and how food hygiene categories differ from planning use classes.

Remote sensing

Green Change Monitor

Web tool · Sentinel-2, NDVI, Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs, geotiff.js

Where were trees and green space lost or gained? Pick an area and two years, and it maps patches of vegetation loss and gain from free Sentinel-2 imagery, entirely in the browser: it finds the clearest scenes, masks clouds, builds a cloud-free greenness map for each year and compares them. In England, patches inside conservation areas and tree preservation areas are flagged so planning and enforcement teams know where to look first. Results download as GeoJSON or CSV. A pointer for where to look, not evidence on its own.
